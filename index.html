How answers are sourced

Each question is matched against the connected sources. A strong match returns an answer with a citation. No match creates a support case with a draft reply for staff to review in Support Cases — the assistant does not send it and does not fill gaps from general knowledge.

Requests to write application material, and questions outside the sources (for example travel or salary questions), are routed to staff the same way.

The four eligibility paths

From the RAC Candidate Guides. You need to meet one combination in full, and attest to it in the application.

EducationRegulatory experience
Bachelor's or equivalentMinimum 3 years
Master's degreeMinimum 2 years
Doctorate degreeMinimum 1 year
No degree pathwayMinimum 10 years

Regulatory-related experience may include quality assurance, quality control, clinical research related to the approval of health products, or health product project management. There are no required prerequisite courses or prior certifications.

Source — RAPS, “Is the Regulatory Affairs Certification right for you?” and the 2026 Candidate Guides.

How scoring works

Pass — answered. The question matched the expected guide section and returned a citation.

Pass — routed. The question is outside the sources, and the assistant created a support case instead of answering.

Fail. Either an in-scope question was routed unnecessarily, or an out-of-scope question was answered. Both are treated as equally serious in a credentialing context.

Why it matters

Coverage is re-checked whenever a guide is updated or a document is added, so a change that breaks an existing answer surfaces here before members see it.
